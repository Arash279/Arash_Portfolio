## Why a review was needed

Fast inverter switching raises several different engineering questions: conducted EMI, voltage stress inside the winding, bearing voltage and condition monitoring. The literature offers many "wideband" models, but they are rarely compared on the question that matters to a designer: *will this model answer my problem?*

## What the review contributes

**A clear map of the field.** Models are sorted on two separate axes: how the motor is represented (lumped circuit, distributed winding model, multiport model) and where the parameters come from (analytical estimates, field simulation, or measurement). Papers often mix these up, which makes fair comparison difficult.

**Practical selection guidance.** Start from the output you need, then pick the simplest model that keeps that information.

| If you need… | Consider… | And test it on… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terminal current / EMI filter design | A terminal circuit or multiport model | Measured current with the real cable and drive |
| Voltage stress inside the winding | A segmented or distributed winding model | Measured voltages at internal points |

**Honest evaluation.** Fitting the terminal impedance, predicting internal behavior and proving system-level performance are three different claims, and each needs its own evidence.

## The key insight in one example

Two models can match the same terminal impedance perfectly yet contain different internal circuits, and so predict different voltages inside the winding. For EMI filter design the terminal fit may be all you need. For insulation design it is not enough; you need a model that keeps the internal nodes, and measurements to check them.
